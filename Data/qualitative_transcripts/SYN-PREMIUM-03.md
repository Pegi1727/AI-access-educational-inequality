## Transcript Dialogue (Synthetic Role-Play)

**Interviewer:** Can you describe the emotional and cognitive impact of navigating restricted AI access?

**Participant:** Even with a working premium account, there is persistent digital fatigue. You spend valuable research time managing connection stability instead of writing.

**Interviewer:** How does this compare to the experience of researchers in unrestricted contexts?

**Participant:** Colleagues abroad can log in seamlessly with university credentials, whereas we navigate multiple digital obstacles just to open the interface.

**Interviewer:** What academic writing advantages do you observe when the premium model functions smoothly?

**Participant:** It offers strong cohesive feedback, suggests appropriate discourse markers, and helps organize multi-part literature reviews systematically.

**Interviewer:** Do you worry about the sustainability of your current premium access?

**Participant:** Constantly. Every billing cycle is a point of vulnerability where third-party payment channels might collapse or change terms unexpectedly.

**Interviewer:** How does that insecurity influence your research planning?

**Participant:** It forces you to rush drafting phases because you never know if your tool availability will disappear next month.

**Interviewer:** What is the core takeaway regarding AI tools and academic equity?

**Participant:** Access to modern educational AI should be treated as fundamental scholarly infrastructure rather than a luxury conditioned on geographic location.
